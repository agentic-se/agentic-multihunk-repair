\subsubsection{Tool Sequence Patterns in Low-Performing Agents}
\label{sec:tool-sequences}

To understand how ineffective repair strategies manifest in agent behavior, we analyze tool sequence patterns using a sliding window approach. We extract consecutive tool invocations of length $k$ from each repair trajectory and count pattern frequencies. Using window size $k=3$, we identify the most common 3-step sequences executed by agents during repair attempts. We focus on the two lowest-performing agents, \qwencode and \geminicli, to characterize distinct failure modes.

Figure~\ref{fig:qwen_gemini_patterns} presents the top five most frequent 3-step tool sequences for each agent, measured as percentages of all 3-step sequences observed in their repair trajectories. \qwencode executed 14,434 total 3-step sequences across 704 unique patterns, while \geminicli executed 12,620 sequences across 324 unique patterns. The distribution reveals fundamentally different behavioral characteristics.

\qwencode exhibits severe navigation overhead. The most frequent pattern, NAVIGATE $\rightarrow$ TEST $\rightarrow$ NAVIGATE, accounts for 3.23\% of all sequences. NAVIGATE operations appear in five of the ten most frequent patterns, indicating that directory traversal consumes a substantial portion of the agent's action budget. Patterns such as WRITE $\rightarrow$ NAVIGATE $\rightarrow$ BUILD and NAVIGATE $\rightarrow$ BUILD $\rightarrow$ NAVIGATE demonstrate that navigation interrupts productive sequences, forcing the agent to repeatedly change directories between operations. This fragmentation correlates with lower repair accuracy, as navigation overhead reduces time available for comprehension and modification activities.

\geminicli demonstrates the opposite failure mode: over-modification without validation. The most frequent pattern, WRITE $\rightarrow$ WRITE $\rightarrow$ WRITE, accounts for 8.74\% of all sequences, representing 2.7 times higher concentration than Qwen's top pattern. Four of the five most frequent patterns begin with WRITE operations, and three involve consecutive writes. This indicates that Gemini batches code modifications without intermediate compilation or testing. The second most frequent pattern, WRITE $\rightarrow$ BUILD $\rightarrow$ TEST (8.65\%), represents the ideal repair cycle, yet the dominance of WRITE $\rightarrow$ WRITE $\rightarrow$ WRITE suggests that the agent frequently deviates from this productive sequence by accumulating modifications before validation.

The contrast between these agents reveals two distinct paths to inefficiency. \qwencode fragments its workflow through excessive navigation, preventing sustained focus on any single repair activity. \geminicli concentrates effort on modification but fails to validate changes incrementally, leading to accumulated errors. Both patterns waste computational resources: Qwen through overhead operations that contribute nothing to repair progress, and Gemini through modifications that may compound errors without feedback. These findings suggest that effective repair strategies require both sustained focus (minimizing navigation overhead) and frequent validation (avoiding excessive modification batches).
